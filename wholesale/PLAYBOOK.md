# Wholesale Playbook (zero-money, owner-listed / 90+ days)

## How the numbers in the deal sheet work

| Step | Formula |
|---|---|
| Est. ARV (value after light repairs) | Median sold $/sqft in the same ZIP over the last 6 months, times the home's sqft. Where no public sale prices exist (all of Texas, plus ZIPs with fewer than 3 sales), I used 95% of the list price. That fallback is a guess: confirm it before you make a firm offer. |
| Est. light repairs | $15/sqft, minimum $8,000 (paint, flooring, fixtures). I could not see photos, so check them. |
| Investor max | 70% x ARV - repairs. This is the most a cash buyer will usually pay. |
| Opening offer | Investor max - $10,000, capped at 85% of the list price |
| Walk-away max | Investor max - $3,500 (your $3,500 minimum fee), never above the list price |
| Grade | A = walk-away is at least 80% of list (small discount needed). B = 65-79%. C = below 65% (long shot, only for very motivated sellers). |

**"Check first" flag:** these homes are priced far below what nearby homes sold for. That usually means a mobile home on rented land, a 55+ community, an HOA restriction, or serious damage. Look at the photos and description before you call.

**Days on Zillow:** Zillow's own "90+ days" and "For Sale By Owner" filters were applied. The tool does not show the exact day count, so glance at the listing to confirm it.

## Step 1: Contact the owner

Zillow does not publish owner emails. Reach owners through the listing: the **Contact / Message owner** button or the phone number on the page. Replies to Zillow messages arrive in your email.

### First message (Zillow message, text, or email)

> Hi [Owner first name], I saw your home at [address] has been listed for a while and wanted to reach out directly. I'm a local real estate investor and I buy houses as-is: no repairs, no cleaning, no agent commissions, and you pick the closing date. Closing would go through a licensed title company, so your money is protected.
>
> Based on recent sales nearby and some updating the house would need, I could offer **$[Opening offer]**. I know that's below your asking price. The difference is that you skip commissions (often 5-6%), repairs, and more months of mortgage, tax, and insurance payments.
>
> Are you open to talking it over? I'm flexible on timing and terms.
>
> [Your name] · [phone] · [mailing address]

### Follow-up if no reply in 2 days

> Hi [Name], just following up on [address]. My offer stands, and I can work around your timeline. If price is the sticking point, what number would make it work for you?

## Step 2: Negotiate (never above the walk-away max)

1. **They say no:** ask "What number would work for you?" Let them name a price first.
2. **Their number is at or below your walk-away:** accept. Go to Step 3.
3. **Their number is above your walk-away:** counter at the midpoint between your opening offer and your walk-away. Add terms that cost you nothing: a closing date they choose, buying as-is, they leave behind anything they don't want, no showings.
4. **Final:** offer your walk-away number once, with a clear "this is the most I can do." If they decline, thank them and check back in 2-3 weeks. Stale sellers often come back.

Do not say you have cash you don't have, and do not invent a deadline. Trust is what gets contracts signed.

## Step 3: Get the contract signed ($0 out of pocket)

1. Call an **investor-friendly title company** in that county and ask: "Do you close assignments, and can you send me your assignable purchase agreement?" Many give one free and open escrow at no charge, because they get paid at closing.
   - Texas: use the free TREC One to Four Family Residential Contract (Resale). Write your buyer name as "[Your name] and/or assigns".
2. The terms to include:
   - Buyer is "[Your name] and/or assigns"
   - **Inspection / due-diligence period of 10-14 days.** This is your legal exit if you can't find a buyer.
   - Closing in 21-30 days, at the title company
   - A **disclosure** that you may assign the contract for a fee. Texas law requires disclosing that you're selling only your contract interest, and other states increasingly require similar disclosures. Ask the title company what your state requires.
3. **Earnest money:** most sellers and title companies expect some ($100-$500 is common on wholesale deals). It stays refundable if you cancel inside the inspection period. If you truly have $0, ask whether earnest money can be due when the contract is assigned. Some sellers accept that.
4. Send the agreement for e-signature with a free tool (Dropbox Sign or DocuSign both have free tiers). **You sign as the buyer.** I can't sign for you.

## Step 4: Find a buyer (same area)

Free places to find cash buyers:
- Local real estate investor association (REIA) meetups and their Facebook groups ("[City] real estate investors")
- Landlords: rental listings in the same ZIP on Zillow and Craigslist (many owners are investors)
- "We buy houses" ads, signs, and websites in that city (they are buyers)
- County records: recent sales to LLCs, which are usually investors
- BiggerPockets forums for that market

### Buyer email / post

> **Off-market deal: [beds]bd/[baths]ba, [sqft] sqft, [City] [ZIP]**
> Price: **$[contract price + your fee]** · Est. ARV: $[ARV] · Est. repairs: $[repairs]
> Closing through [title company] by [date]. Access for walkthrough: [dates].
> I am selling my contract interest in this property (assignment). I am not a licensed agent. Proof of funds required.
> First signed assignment with earnest money gets it.

Price for the buyer: start at investor max (70% rule). Your fee = buyer price - seller contract price. Never accept a fee below $3,500.

## Step 5: Close and get paid

1. Buyer signs the **Assignment of Contract** (the title company has one) and deposits their earnest money with the title company.
2. The title company runs title, pays the seller, and **pays your assignment fee at closing**.
3. **Timeline:** contract to closing usually takes 2-4 weeks. A contract signed this week pays in December, not by Friday.
