# Playbook: anatomy of the reference reel

Source: Instagram reel `DfxzckOMkSY` (178 s, 1080×1920, 30 fps, VP9/AAC), a sports-comparison explainer.
Claim: *"Stephen Curry is basketball's Lionel Messi. LeBron James is its Cristiano Ronaldo."*
Below is what happens on screen and why it works, so the structure can be reused for any topic.
Quoted phrases are from the reference and show the pattern; don't copy them into new videos.

## Numbers

| Metric | Value |
|---|---|
| Duration | 178 s (Instagram allows up to 3 min) |
| Layouts (scenes) | ~16, so one every ~11 s |
| On-screen element pops | ~110, one every ~1.6 s |
| Narration pace | ~165–175 wpm, sentences of 3–9 words |
| Audio | voice only, no music bed; −14.1 LUFS, LRA 3.2 LU, true peak −1.2 dBTP |
| Hard cuts | only 6 (all around the ad break); everything else builds inside layouts |
| Captions | 1–2 lines, ≤ 6 words, lime box on the spoken word |

## Persistent elements

- Top-left brand row (logo tile + name), series tags (`STEPH IS MESSI` mustard | `LEBRON IS RONALDO` red), chapter chip `1 / 4` + lime label.
- Vertical photo-credit strip on the right edge: "Photos (edited): Author, CC BY 2.0 · …".
- Faint hand-drawn court sketch at the bottom, behind the captions.

## Why it holds attention

1. **Claim in frame 0.** The thumbnail is the argument.
2. **A promised structure** (roadmap tags + chapter chip) tells the viewer how long they're in for.
3. **Constant change**: something new appears on almost every sentence, with no static stretches over ~2 s.
4. **Fair fight**: each side wins a category, so the comments argue instead of dismissing it.
5. **Receipts on screen**: sources, dates and photo credits make it feel trustworthy and shareable.
6. **Loop ending**: the last layout restates the first.
7. **Forced-choice CTA** in the caption ("Magician or engine: who are you taking?") drives comments.

## Adapting to other topics

The equation works whenever two pairs share a structure: two rivalries, two companies vs two
companies, two artists, two cities. Keep the same skeleton: claim → lenses (3–4 chapters) → bridge →
mindset → rivalry evidence → twist → loop close.
